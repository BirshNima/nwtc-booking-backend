Paid search & corporate growth

Step 10. Paid search buys the demand that already exists while SEO builds; the corporate motion turns one-off riders into monthly recurring revenue. Both run on the same principle as the rest of the system — nothing scales until it's measured to a booking.

v1 · 2026-09-02Channels: Google Ads · LSA · Microsoft Ads · B2B outreachDepends on: conversion tracking (§8)

01 Strategy

Paid is for now; SEO is for later

The SEO plan takes 2–12 months to produce leads. Paid search produces them this week — at a cost. Run paid to cover the gap and to buy the highest-intent clicks SEO may never rank #1 for, then let organic take over the terms it can win and shift budget to the next opportunity.

Tracking first, spend second

Do not turn on a campaign until §8 is done and verified. Smart Bidding optimizes toward whatever you tell it is a conversion; if that's "landed on the page" you'll pay for traffic, and if it's a real booking you'll pay for revenue. The difference is the whole game.

One campaign, prove it, then expand

Start with Airport only — highest intent, most predictable value (flat rates), clearest ad message. Run it 30 days. If cost per qualified lead lands in range, add the next campaign. If it doesn't, fix the funnel or the targeting before spending more.

Access needed

A Google Ads account, a Google Business Profile (for Local Services Ads and location extensions), GA4 + Google Tag Manager, and a call-tracking account. Local Services Ads also need license (WUTC) and insurance verification — start that process early, it takes weeks.

Settings that apply to every campaign

  • Networks: Search only. No Display, no Search Partners at launch.
  • Location: King + Pierce counties + the North Sound cities you serve. Target "presence" (people in the area), not "presence or interest" — you don't want someone in Ohio researching Seattle.
  • Language: English. Ad schedule: all hours (dispatch is 24/7) — bid-adjust up for the booking-heavy windows once data shows them.
  • Devices: no exclusions; mobile is where the calls come from. Ensure the sticky call button is unmissable on the landing pages.
  • Match types: phrase + exact at launch. Broad match only later, with Smart Bidding and a mature negative list.

04 Google Local Services Ads

A separate product from search ads, and for a licensed local operator it's often the best ROI channel. LSA units sit at the very top of results on mobile, show your rating and "Google Screened" badge, and you pay per lead (a call or message), not per click. Disputable leads (wrong number, spam, out of area) can be refunded.

To launch

  1. Apply under the transportation / limo & car service category.
  2. Submit business license (WUTC operating authority) and proof of commercial insurance.
  3. Complete the background-check process for the business and any named drivers Google requires.
  4. Set the service area to your cities and the services (airport, corporate, events, hourly).
  5. Set a weekly budget = target leads × expected cost per lead ($25–55 for transportation locally). Start at ~5–8 leads/week.
  6. Turn on the connection to your Google reviews — LSA rank is heavily review-driven, which is why §8 of the SEO plan (2–4 reviews/week) matters here too.

Answer LSA calls fast and every time — Google demotes advertisers with low answer rates. Route them to the 24/7 dispatch line. Mark lead quality in the LSA dashboard so the algorithm learns.

08 Conversion tracking — the gate

Nothing in §3 runs until this chain is built and a test booking has flowed all the way through.

ad click (gclid) → landing page → funnel event → CRM lead (gclid stored) → booked / completed → offline conversion + value back to Google Ads
  1. GA4 + GTM live on the site. The funnel already emits quote_start, quote_complete, booking_reserved, quote_request, phone_click — wire these as GA4 events.
  2. Mark conversions in GA4: quote_complete and booking_reserved as primary; quote_request and phone_click as secondary. Import them into Google Ads (or use Google Ads tags directly).
  3. Enhanced conversions for web — pass hashed email/phone from the funnel's confirmation step so conversions survive cookie loss.
  4. Call tracking — Google forwarding numbers on call assets and call-only ads (counts calls over 60s as conversions). Add CallRail (or similar) with dynamic number insertion for the website number so organic vs. paid calls are separated.
  5. Store the gclid — funnel captures it from the URL, includes it in payload; intake.js writes it to a GCLID field on the Lead.
  6. Offline conversion import — when a CRM lead reaches Booked (and again at Completed with the fare as the value), upload the conversion against its gclid to Google Ads via a Make/Zapier step or the API. Now Smart Bidding optimizes toward booked revenue, not form fills.
  7. Verify end to end — click your own ad, complete a booking, confirm it lands in GA4, in the CRM with the gclid, and (after the import runs) in Google Ads as a conversion with a value.
Why offline import matters here

Airport quote-completions and corporate form fills vary wildly in value — a $79 Kent run vs. a 40-trip-a-month account. Feeding the actual booked value back lets the algorithm bid up for the searches that produce real money and down for the ones that produce tire-kickers. Without it you're optimizing for lead volume, which is how paid budgets get wasted.

09 Microsoft Ads (Bing)

  • Import the Google campaigns once they're stable (~week 4), then let it auto-sync weekly.
  • Bid −10 to −20% versus Google to start; adjust off performance.
  • Budget ~20–30% of the Google spend.
  • Bing's audience skews older, higher-income, more desktop, and more corporate/government — the Corporate campaign often returns a better cost per account here than on Google. Give it a proportionally larger share of the Microsoft budget.
  • Re-import the negative lists; set up UET tag + conversion tracking mirroring GA4.

10 Budget & phasing

All figures are monthly and contingent on §8 being live and the first 30 days showing a workable cost per booked ride. Local transportation CPCs run roughly $4–10 (airport), $8–18 (corporate/limo), higher for the broad "limo service" head terms — validate in Keyword Planner.

Tier~ MonthlyRunning
Lean / test$1,000–1,500Airport + Branded search, + Local Services Ads. One channel to prove the funnel.
Standard$2,500–3,500+ Corporate + Wedding (in season) + Hourly/wine + Microsoft import.
Growth$5,000–7,000+ General / brand-category, broader match with Smart Bidding, expanded LSA budget, bigger Microsoft share.

Scaling rules

  • Raise a campaign's budget by ≤ 20% at a time, no more than weekly — bigger jumps reset Smart Bidding's learning.
  • Only scale a campaign whose cost per booked ride (from the offline import) is at or below target and stable for 2+ weeks.
  • Move budget toward the campaigns and ad groups with the best cost per booking, not evenly.
  • Pause any ad group with spend > 3× target CPA and zero conversions after 30 days; diagnose before relaunching.

11 Corporate growth

One corporate account can be worth more than a month of airport ads. Ads capture the corporate searchers who are already looking; this motion creates demand among the ones who aren't.

The motion

Mirror the CRM's corporate pipeline: Prospect → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Account Created → Active.

  1. Partnership-first (fastest volume). Pitch hotel concierge desks, wedding/event venues, and Alaska-cruise travel agencies to be their recommended transportation. Warm referrals convert far better than cold outreach and need no ad spend.
  2. Direct outreach. Build a list of 100 Tier A/B companies. LinkedIn connect + a value-first message to the EA / Office Manager, then a short email sequence. Templates in outreach-templates.md.
  3. In person. Join and show up at the Bellevue and Seattle Metro chambers, and a local Executive Assistant / IAAP chapter. Sponsor one event a quarter.
  4. Referral program. Existing corporate clients get an account credit for any referred company that activates an account.

The offer

  • No setup fee, no minimum volume to start.
  • A one-month pilot at the Preferred tier (10% off) so they can test the service on real trips.
  • Certificate of insurance and WUTC operating authority provided up front.
  • A named contact and the dedicated dispatch line from day one.
  • Monthly consolidated invoice, Net 15.

Cadence & targets

40 new contacts / wk → ~15% reply → ~4 meetings / wk → ~1 proposal / wk → ~1 account / 2–3 wks

A 5-touch sequence over ~3 weeks: LinkedIn connect → LinkedIn message (day 2) → email 1 (day 4) → email 2 (day 9) → break-up email (day 18). Stop the moment they reply or book. Everyone who doesn't convert goes to a quarterly re-touch.

Track in the CRM: contacts, replies, meetings, proposals sent, accounts activated, and monthly recurring revenue added — that last number is the goal.

Days 1–30 tracking & test

Get measurable, launch one campaign, start the corporate list.

  • Conversion tracking §8 fully built and verified with a live test booking.
  • Start LSA verification (license + insurance + background checks).
  • Launch Airport + Branded search. Maximize Conversions. $35–50/day combined.
  • Add gclid capture to the funnel + intake.js.
  • Build the 100-company corporate list; draft the outreach templates; pitch 10 hotels/venues.

Days 31–60 expand what works

Add campaigns if the airport numbers hold; run outreach batch one.

  • Review airport cost per booked ride. If in range: launch Corporate + Wedding (if in season) + Hourly/wine.
  • LSA live once verified; start at 5–8 leads/week.
  • Import to Microsoft Ads.
  • Corporate outreach batch one (40 contacts/week); first pilot proposals; follow up on partnership pitches.

Days 61–90 optimize & decide

First real read on blended acquisition cost; set the next quarter's budget.

  • First full combined scorecard. Compare paid vs. organic vs. corporate cost per new customer.
  • Shift budget toward the best-performing campaigns; pause the worst ad groups.
  • Launch General / brand-category only if airport CPA is proven and there's budget to learn.
  • Corporate: first accounts activated; launch the referral program to them.
  • Decide the ongoing monthly budget tier (§10) from the data, not a guess.
