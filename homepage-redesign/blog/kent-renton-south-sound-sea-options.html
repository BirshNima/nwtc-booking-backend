Airport travel

Kent, Renton & the South Sound: your SEA airport options

The South King County cities are the closest thing to Sea-Tac in the whole region. Here's how each one connects, and what a car service adds over driving yourself.

Northwest Town Car Service · updated September 2026 · 5 min read

From Kent, Renton, Federal Way, and Auburn, Sea-Tac is a 12–30 minute drive and a flat car-service rate of $79–$89 for a sedan. Because these cities are so close, the value of a chauffeur isn't a long comfortable ride — it's not paying $30+ a day to leave a car in the SEA garage, not driving home exhausted after a red-eye, and a firm pickup time for the 5 a.m. flight.

Kent — the shortest run

12–18 minutes off-peak, 20–30 in rush. From West Hill and downtown, Kent–Des Moines Road (SR‑516) to SR‑509 is often faster than the freeway; from East Hill it's 104th to SR‑516 to I‑5. Flat rate: $79 sedan, $109 SUV. We're based in Kent, so a car is usually close for same-day trips. Kent car service →

Renton — I‑405 and the S‑curves

15–20 minutes off-peak, 25–35 in rush. I‑405 south to SR‑518, or Rainier Avenue and SR‑509 when the freeway is jammed. The catch is that the I‑405 Renton S‑curves — the regional bottleneck — start right here, so from the Highlands we drop onto 405 further south. Flat rate: $79 sedan, $109 SUV. Renton car service →

Federal Way — north on I‑5, ahead of the crowd

20–25 minutes off-peak, 30–45 on a weekday morning when the northbound I‑5 stretch through Kent slows. SR‑99 (Pacific Highway South) is the stoplight-heavy alternative. Flat rate: $85 sedan, $115 SUV. Federal Way car service →

Auburn — the valley routes

25–30 minutes off-peak, 35–50 in rush. SR‑18 west to I‑5 north, or A Street to SR‑167 to I‑405 and SR‑518. The SR‑18/I‑5 interchange near Federal Way is the usual slow point. Flat rate: $89 sedan, $119 SUV. Auburn also has a heavy warehouse-district corporate base — Auburn car service →

The case for a car from this close

When the drive is 20 minutes, people assume it's not worth booking a service. But the math still works: SEA parking runs $30–45 a day, so a week-long trip is $200+ before you've factored in the walk from the garage with bags. A one-way car at $79–89 each direction is comparable and drops you at the door. Add a 5 a.m. flight, a red-eye return, or a group with luggage, and it's not close. For frequent flyers, a corporate or personal account makes it routine.
