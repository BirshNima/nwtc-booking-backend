Booking system blueprint

How the website becomes a customer-acquisition and booking system: the pipeline from ad click to repeat fare, the data it runs on, every automation and where a human signs off, the tools that do the work, and the order to build them in.

v1 · 2026-09-01 Owner: business owner + implementer Status: design, pre-implementation

01 Overview

Today the site is a brochure: a visitor reads it, maybe calls, and everything after that lives in a phone and an inbox. This blueprint turns it into a system that captures every lead, prices and follows up automatically where that's safe, hands the risky calls to a person, and learns from its own numbers.

Two principles

1. Automate what's reliable; gate what's risky. Confirmations, reminders, and review requests run untouched. Unusual quotes, refunds, late cancellations, driver changes, corporate terms, and any reply to a complaint wait for a one-tap human approval before they go out. Every workflow below is tagged automated or approval gate.

2. Build in phases. A half-built system that never loses a lead beats a fully-specified one that ships in six months. Section 7 sequences it so value lands in week 4.

What "done" looks like

  • Every enquiry — form, call, or online booking — creates one record in one place, tagged with where it came from.
  • A booking confirms, takes a deposit, and schedules its own reminders without anyone touching it.
  • A quote that isn't accepted chases itself four times, then stops the moment the customer books.
  • A finished trip asks for a review; a great review is routed to Google, a poor one to the owner.
  • The owner opens one dashboard and sees today's trips, unpaid bookings, stale quotes, and where revenue moved.
How to read this

Sections 2–6 are the design — hand them to whoever implements. Section 7 is the plan. Section 9 lists the decisions only you can make; nothing gets built until those are settled.

02 The pipeline

Traffic arrives from search, maps, social, ads, and referrals, and lands on a service or location page. From there it forks: a small share book immediately, most ask for a quote or call. Both paths meet again at dispatch, and everything after the trip is a loop designed to produce the next booking.

Lead status — the pipeline every enquiry moves through

New → Contacted → Quote Sent → Follow-up → Booked → Paid → Completed → (Review) — with Cancelled and Lost as exits. A Lead that reaches Booked is now driven by its Booking status; the two shouldn't disagree.

Corporate pipeline — a separate track

Prospect → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Account Created → Active → Inactive. Corporate deals move on weeks, not hours, so they get their own follow-up cadence and their own view in the dashboard.

04 Automation workflows

Each workflow below names its trigger, its steps, whether a human approves anything, who owns it, and what happens when a step fails. Every one of them inherits the same safety rig:

Shared safety rig — applies to all workflows
  • Duplicate protection — match on phone + pickup date before creating any Lead, Customer, or Booking; update instead of inserting.
  • Retry — transient failures (email/SMS/API timeouts) retry 3× with backoff, then raise an alert.
  • Failure alert — any unrecovered failure texts/emails the owner with the record link and the error.
  • Logging — every send, status change, and approval is written to an activity log on the record.
  • Manual override — every automated step can be paused, skipped, or re-sent by hand from the CRM.

New quote request / inbound call

automated gate on unusual quotes
Trigger
Website quote form, "request a quote" from the funnel, or a logged call.
Owner
System creates; owner prices exceptions.
On failure
Form submissions also email a copy to the booking inbox as a backstop.
  1. Create Lead (status New), capture source + campaign from the URL.
  2. Send an instant "we've got your request" acknowledgement with expected response time.
  3. If the trip fits the standard rate table (known city pair, standard vehicle, ≤ set distance): generate the Quote automatically, move to Quote Sent.
  4. If it doesn't (events, multi-vehicle, out-of-area, custom): route to the owner as approve / adjust; send only after a tap.
  5. Start the quote follow-up sequence.

Abandoned booking recovery

approve copy once, then automated
Trigger
Booking engine captured contact details but no payment within 60 minutes.
Owner
Owner approves the message template once; sends run automatically after.
On failure / caution
Only fire if an email or phone was captured with consent. One message, not a sequence. Never for the same person twice in 30 days.
  1. Create Lead (status New, tag "abandoned").
  2. Wait 1 hour; if still unpaid, send one helpful message: "want a hand finishing this? reply or call."
  3. If no response in 48h, fold into the normal follow-up sequence at Day 3.

Pre-trip reminders & instructions

automated
Trigger
Scheduled from booking, relative to pickup time.
Owner
System
On failure
If SMS fails, fall back to email and flag the trip for a manual call.

  2. Day-of / 3h before: driver name, vehicle, plate, and direct contact once assigned.
  3. Airport pickups: include terminal/meeting-point instructions, the flight number on file, curbside vs. meet-and-greet, and the dispatch number. Adjust the send time if flight tracking shows a delay.

Driver assignment / dispatch

human assigns notifications automated
Trigger
New trip on the calendar, or a change to an assigned trip.
Owner

On failure
Unassigned trips inside 12h of pickup escalate — repeated alert until assigned.

  2. Driver is notified with trip sheet; must accept.
  3. On accept, the day-of reminder to the customer unlocks the driver's details.

Trip completion → thank-you → review

automated gate on low ratings
Trigger
Driver marks the trip complete (or the pickup time passes + no dispute).
Owner
System; owner handles anything under 4★.
  1. Set Trip completed, charge any balance/gratuity, send the receipt.
  2. Wait 2–3 hours, send a short thank-you.
  3. Next morning: ask for a 1–5 rating (one tap in the message).
  4. 4–5★: thank them, link straight to Google/Yelp review.
  5. 1–3★: do not ask for a public review. Create a task for the owner to call within 24h owner-owned.
  6. Record review status on the Customer.

Segmentation & repeat booking

automated
Trigger
Trip completion updates the Customer's segment nightly.
Owner
System; owner approves any new campaign template.
  1. Re-segment: airport regular, corporate, wedding/event, one-time, lapsed (no trip in 120 days).
  2. Airport regulars: light touch before common travel periods.
  3. Lapsed at 60 days: friendly check-in. At 120: a stronger win-back offer offer approved once.
  4. All campaign sends honor marketing opt-in and include one-tap unsubscribe.

human-led

Cancellation & refund

always human-approved
Trigger
Customer requests a cancellation, or the owner cancels a trip.
Owner
Owner
  1. System shows the cancellation policy, time-to-pickup, and amount paid.
  2. Owner decides: full refund / partial / fee retained, and approves.
  3. System processes the refund, updates Booking + Payment, sends confirmation, frees the calendar slot.

Payment failure

automated escalates to human
Trigger
Deposit or balance charge declines.
Owner
System retries; owner intervenes after.
  1. Retry once after a short delay.
  2. If still failing, message the customer with a secure link to update the card.
  3. Booking held (not cancelled) for 24h; owner alerted to follow up before releasing the slot.

05 Automate vs. approve

The dividing line: automate anything where a wrong send is cheap to fix and rare. Gate anything that moves money, changes a commitment, or speaks for the business in a delicate moment.

Runs automatically, end to endHuman approves, system then executes
Booking & payment confirmations, receiptsQuotes outside the standard rate table — events, multi-vehicle, out-of-area, > set distance
Pre-trip reminders (24h, day-of)Refunds, credits, fare adjustments, goodwill gestures
Airport pickup instructions, flight-aware timingCancellations inside the penalty window
Thank-you messages; review requests for 4–5★Driver reassignment messaging to the customer
Lead → CRM record, source/campaign taggingAny reply to a complaint or a 1–3★ review
Quote follow-up sequence (day 0/1/3/7)Corporate contract terms, rates, and payment terms
Nightly re-segmentationFirst send of any new campaign or template
Repeat / win-back campaigns from approved templatesAbandoned-booking outreach copy (approve once, then automated)

Approval mechanics

A gate should be one tap on a phone — a message with Approve / Edit / Decline, or a task in the CRM app. If nothing is approved within a set window (e.g. 2h for quotes during business hours), it re-alerts rather than sending on its own. As volume grows, individual rules can graduate from "gate" to "auto" once they've proven reliable — that's a deliberate decision, logged, not a default.

Rough all-in: assembled-from-parts, low volume: ~$150–350/mo plus payment fees and SMS usage. An all-in-one limo platform replaces the booking engine, dispatch, driver app, flight tracking, and some CRM for ~$100–300/mo, at the cost of less control over the data model.

Compliance to budget for

US business SMS now requires A2P 10DLC registration (brand + campaign) before Twilio or any provider will deliver reliably — a few weeks of lead time and a small registration fee. Also: PCI stays Stripe's problem only if card data never touches your servers (use Stripe Checkout / Payment Links, not custom card fields), and marketing messages need clear consent capture + opt-out.

07 Phased rollout

Each phase is independently useful. Don't start a phase until the previous one is running cleanly.

Phase 1 weeks 1–4

Stop losing leads. Every enquiry lands in one place and gets an instant acknowledgement.

Scope

  • Booking & quote funnel live on a "Book Online" page, wired to a lead sink.
  • CRM set up with the data model; every form + call creates a Lead with source tagging.
  • Automated: instant acknowledgement, owner notification, booking confirmation email/SMS.
  • Dispatch stays manual — Google Calendar + driver texts. Quoting stays manual.
  • Call tracking number in place so phone leads are attributed.

Add

CRM · Make/Zapier · Twilio (start 10DLC now) · Resend/Postmark · CallRail · GA4 + Search Console

Done when

You can't name a lead from the last two weeks that isn't in the CRM, and every one got a reply within minutes.

Phase 2 weeks 5–10

Money and reminders run themselves. Quotes chase; trips confirm; reviews come in.

Scope

  • Booking engine + Stripe deposits (build on the funnel, or stand up Moovs/Limo Anywhere).
  • Standard-rate auto-quoting; unusual quotes routed to the owner's approval gate.
  • Quote follow-up sequence (day 0/1/3/7, stop on booking).
  • Pre-trip reminder workflow, including airport instructions + flight-aware timing.
  • Trip completion → thank-you → review request, with the 1–3★ gate.
  • Abandoned-booking recovery (one message).

Add

Stripe · booking engine · flight tracking · review routing

Done when

A weekday with no owner input still produces confirmed bookings, sent reminders, and at least one review request.

Phase 3 weeks 11+

The system grows the business, not just runs it.

Scope

  • Nightly segmentation; repeat + win-back campaigns from approved templates.
  • Corporate pipeline automation — owner-nudge reminders, quarterly check-ins, monthly invoicing.
  • Admin dashboard (Section 8) and the monthly performance report.
  • The AI-assisted optimization loop — recommendations reviewed by a human before anything changes.
  • Driver app / self-serve dispatch if not already via the platform.

Add

Looker Studio dashboard · campaign tooling · invoicing

Done when

The monthly report writes most of itself, and you're making changes based on its numbers rather than hunches.

08 Dashboards & the optimization loop

Admin dashboard — the daily view

One screen, opened every morning:

  • Today & tomorrow's trips — with assignment status; unassigned ones flagged.
  • New leads (last 24–48h) and pending quotes awaiting approval or customer response.
  • Unpaid bookings and held bookings (payment failed).
  • Stale quotes — sent > 3 days ago, no response.
  • Corporate leads by stage; anything untouched > 5 business days.
  • This week: bookings, revenue, cancellations, completed trips.
  • Reviews needing a response.

Monthly performance report

Generated from GA4 + Search Console + the CRM + Stripe. Answers the twelve questions you listed: what moved revenue up and down, which services and locations are growing or shrinking, which pages produce bookings vs. traffic with no leads, which keywords and campaigns bring paying customers, what technical issues and reviews need attention, and what to test next. Ends in a Top 10 priorities list — each with problem, evidence, recommended action, expected impact, difficulty, owner, deadline, and the metric that proves it worked. It maintains a running backlog rather than proposing rebuilds.

The AI-assisted optimization loop

  1. Monthly, the data is pulled into a structured summary (no raw dumping).
  2. An analysis pass identifies patterns, anomalies, and candidate experiments against the backlog.
  3. It drafts the Top 10 with evidence attached.
  4. A human reviews and picks what to actually do — this is a gate, not an autopilot.
  5. Chosen changes are made, tagged with a start date, and measured against their success metric next cycle.
Guardrail

The loop never changes pricing, ad spend, page content, or automations on its own. It recommends; you approve; the change is logged so next month's report can measure it.
